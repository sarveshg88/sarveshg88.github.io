The insight

The gap isn’t people. It’s readiness.

The route asks for three tests. None of them checks whether a candidate can handle day one on a Japanese care floor.

Test 1Japanese languageJFT‑Basic or JLPT N4
Test 2Care skillsNursing Care Skills Evaluation Test
Test 3Care JapaneseA third of it is care documents
Kizuna focuses hereDay one on the care floorCalming a resident who refuses care. Reporting a fall to the shift leader. Writing a factual care record. In Japanese.

Passing three tests isn’t the same as calming a resident who refuses care, or reporting a fall in Japanese.

The product

Three moments, practised until they pass.

Trainees practise a scenario, get an instant score against the centre’s rubric, retry, and the results roll up into a readiness band for trainers and the centre.

A

Respond

A resident refuses care, is confused or distressed. Respond with respect and the right Japanese.

今日はお風呂、入りたくないなあ…I don’t feel like a bath today…
そうなんですね。では、足湯から始めてみませんか？I see. Shall we start with a warm foot bath?
Feedback: refusal acknowledged first; ませんか makes it an offer, not an order.
B

Report

Something goes wrong. Give the shift leader a clear four part report.

What happened · time and what you found
Condition · conscious, where it hurts
Action · not moved, staying with her
Help needed · a polite request for a nurse
Observed, not assumed · “she fell” is flagged when she was only found on the floor
C

Document

At the end of a shift, turn what happened into a factual care record. Opinions get flagged.

10:15 Offered a bath. Resident said 「今日は入りたくない」. 10:30 Offered a foot bath; she agreed. She was being difficult today. Flagged: a judgement, not a fact. Record her own words instead.

Business model Proposed

Centres pay. Trainees use it free.

One buyer, two users. The price is deliberately not fixed: the pilot sets it.

Main revenue

Centres pay

A per learner licence for the platform and trainer dashboard, so they can decide who is ready for placement, with evidence.

Second payer

Employers fund seats

Overseas employers already invest heavily in each foreign carer. Sponsored seats show them each hire’s readiness score.

Access

Trainees free

No cost to the carer, so the whole batch practises, not just those who can pay.

First pilot · concierge MVP · proposed

One training centre, one batch, all three modes.

Results reviewed with the trainers by hand, and a readiness report handed to the centre for each candidate. First centres to approach: TOMCOM (Telangana) and ODEPC (Kerala), both already preparing carers for Japan.

The pilot answers three questions
  1. Do trainees improve with practice?
  2. Do trainers trust the readiness score?
  3. Will the centre pay to continue?

Product judgement

What I chose not to claim.

Every position is stated as context, a design choice or a proposal, never as a measured result. A judge who catches one shaky number distrusts the rest.

“135,000” is a ceiling, not jobs

It is the planned maximum intake for all nationalities over five years. Never “135,000 vacancies”, never “for Indians”.

No quit prediction dashboard

Considered and left out: no evidence behind it, consent and fairness problems, and it would break the promise that practice is safe.

No price, no market size in rupees

Early drafts had per learner prices and a revenue figure. They came out: the pilot is how we learn what a centre will actually pay.

Alternatives, not rivals

Claims about what named companies lack couldn’t be verified, so the comparison is against classes, generic AI tools and specialist centres. Kizuna complements teachers; it doesn’t replace them.
